Paano MaglaroHow to Play

The bangka is a boat, not a car

WASD or the arrow keys steer. The hull carries momentum and water drag, so it swings wide into a turn and glides for a moment after you let go. Lead your turns.

Shift sprints and drains the stamina bar. It refills on its own, slowly — or instantly from a packed Tubig.

Current

Some flooded streets run fast. You will see the streaks in the water before you feel the push — those lanes shove the boat sideways, and fighting one costs stamina you may want later.

Debris

Hit floating debris at speed and the boat staggers. If people are aboard, one can go over the side. You can pick them back up, but the clock does not stop while you do.

The loop

Pull alongside a resident and press Space or E. Not everyone can be taken the same way:

SituationWhat you need
On a roof or in a treeNothing — just get there
In open waterSalbabida (lifebuoy)
Trapped behind debrisLubid (rope)
Injured 🩹Botika (first-aid) — a 2-second hold to stabilise first

Triage

An icon floats above every resident: 👵 elderly · 🧒 child · 🩹 injured · 🧍 able-bodied. The first three score higher and run out of time faster. A ring around them drains as their situation worsens — when it empties, someone on a roof slips into the water, and someone already in the water is gone.

The boat holds three people. When it is full you must run them to the evacuation center before you can take anyone else. That ferry rhythm is the whole game — who you take first genuinely matters.

Drop-off

Steer into the marked water in front of the evacuation center and everyone aboard is unloaded automatically. Hold E there for 1.5s to call it in and end the mission early — worth it if the water is about to take the rest.

Packed before you leave, not during

The Preparation Phase gives you four slots (five on the later missions) and eight things worth taking. You cannot restock mid-mission. That is the point.

Whatever you packed appears in the hotbar at the bottom of the screen, numbered 1 to 5. Some are passive and simply work; the rest you trigger:

  • Flashlight — toggles the beam. Widens and lengthens the visibility cone enormously at dusk and at night.
  • Tubig — three swigs, each refilling stamina instantly.
  • Radyo — pings the direction of the nearest resident you have not found, then cools down for 20 seconds.
Nothing is a hard fail. Missing the radio makes Mission 2 much harder, not impossible — listen for the calls for help instead. They get louder as you close in.

The clock is the flood

There is no countdown timer. Instead the waterline climbs, tracked by the vertical gauge on the right. As it rises it swallows the low roofs one by one — and anyone still standing on a roof when it goes under is lost.

Past the dashed CRITICAL mark the rain thickens, the sky drops and the audio turns. The mission ends when the gauge tops out, when everyone has been rescued or lost, or when you call it in from the evacuation center.

A mission played reasonably runs two to four minutes. Move.

Tungkol DitoAbout this project

Bayanihan

The word names a Filipino tradition: the neighbours gather, slide bamboo poles under a bahay kubo, and physically carry the house to its new place. Nobody is paid. Nobody could do it alone. It is the picture on the title screen, and it is the argument the game is making — rescue is a community act, and preparation is what makes it possible.

SDG 11 — Sustainable Cities and Communities. Target 11.5 is about substantially reducing the deaths and the number of people affected by disasters, with a direct focus on the poor and on people in vulnerable situations. The Philippines takes roughly twenty tropical cyclones a year. This game is one small piece of the preparedness half of that target.

Why a game

Philippine disaster-preparedness education is delivered almost entirely through text-heavy modules, seminars and drills that students disengage from. The content here is the same content — go-bag contents, triage priority, evacuation procedure, the cost of being unprepared — but it arrives as consequence instead of as a slide. A missing flashlight is a missing person you do not find in time.

The go-bag this is modelled on

The Preparation Phase is a compressed version of the household emergency kit recommended by Philippine disaster-risk agencies. The real checklist is longer than four slots, which is exactly why choosing hurts:

  • Drinking water — about 3 litres per person per day, for three days
  • Non-perishable food and a manual can opener
  • First-aid kit plus any maintenance medicines
  • Flashlight and spare batteries — not your phone torch, which you will need for its battery
  • Battery or hand-crank radio, for bulletins when the towers are down
  • Whistle, rope, and a lifebuoy or flotation aid if you live near water
  • Raincoat, dry clothes, blanket
  • Photocopies of IDs and documents in a sealed plastic bag
  • Cash in small denominations
Take one thing away from this: the bag is packed on a quiet Tuesday, not when the water is at the door. Every choice this game makes you regret is a choice that is free to make correctly right now.

Build notes

Vertical slice — three missions, roughly ten to fifteen minutes end to end. Plain HTML5, CSS3 and vanilla JavaScript with a Canvas2D arena; no engine, no build step, no server, no localStorage. Settings and progress live in memory for the session.

Art note: the reference art delivered in BAYANIHAN_ELEMENTS/ belongs to a different project (Terraqua Clash), so every scene, sprite and icon here is drawn procedurally — the palette and layout are taken from that reference, the content is not.
