Survival Guide

The one-sentence version

Shove every other animal off the island before the timer runs out — while the island itself keeps turning from land into water underneath you.

Winning a round

  • There is no health and no damage. The whole fight is positioning: barge rivals into deep water, or strand them in a biome that drains them.
  • You are out if you fall into deep water (the dark blue), or if your stamina ring empties while you are stuck in the wrong biome.
  • Last animal standing wins immediately. If the timer expires with several still alive, the win goes to the highest score — survival time plus knockouts you caused.
  • In the closing seconds the sea level rises for good and the island erodes from its edges inward, so a stalemate cannot last.

Shoving

Every collision transfers a push scaled by your Push stat and how fast you were moving. Hold a direction and slam into someone to nudge them; use Dash for a committed hit that sends light animals flying. Dash has a short cooldown, and missing leaves you skidding — so it is a real risk near an edge.

What Tide-Shift actually does

The arena is not a flat platform with random puddles. Every tile has a fixed elevation, and the match has a single sea level that rises and falls in a slow cycle. Land is simply everything currently above that line.

Because the whole coastline moves at once, high ridges stay dry the longest and low flats flood first — so the map has real geography you can learn and play around, rather than tiles flipping at random.

Watch the banner. Two seconds before the water turns, the arena announces TIDE RISING or TIDE RECEDING and the weather changes to match. That telegraph is your window to reach the right ground.

Biome advantage

  • Land animals move at full speed on land and lose stamina in water.
  • Water animals are fastest in water and lose stamina on dry land.
  • Amphibious animals never drain, but never get the speed bonus either.

Your stamina ring sits under your feet. Empty it and you are eliminated — so being caught in the wrong biome is just as lethal as being pushed off the edge, and a shove that strands someone counts as a kill in slow motion.

Rising seas

For the last 30 seconds the floor of the tide climbs permanently. Ground that used to resurface never does. The island shrinks from the outside in until there is barely a ridge left.

Four pickups

They spawn on the island every few seconds and expire if nobody grabs them. Walk over one to use it instantly.

    Only one of each effect can run on you at a time, and picking up a second copy refreshes the timer rather than stacking it.

    Why this game exists

    Most survival games are entertainment only. The concepts in SDG 14 (Life Below Water) and SDG 15 (Life on Land) are usually taught with static, text-heavy material that struggles to hold a younger audience. Terraqua Clash puts the idea in the mechanic instead of in a paragraph.

    SDG 14 — Life Below Water. Rising and encroaching water is not scenery here; it is the thing that kills you. Marine species thrive exactly where terrestrial ones start drowning, which is the point: the two systems are not separate.
    SDG 15 — Life On Land. Every habitat in Terrain Select is a real ecosystem under real pressure, and the endgame — ground that floods and never comes back — is habitat loss rendered as a game rule.

    What a player should walk away with

    • Marine and terrestrial ecosystems are interdependent, not parallel.
    • Specialists lose ground when a habitat changes faster than they can adapt — the generalist on the roster is the one who copes.
    • Habitat loss is rarely a single event. It is a slow ratchet, which is why the tide floor only ever climbs.
